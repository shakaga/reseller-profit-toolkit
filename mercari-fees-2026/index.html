Mercari Fees 2026: One 10% Fee — With a Catch

The short answer: Mercari charges sellers exactly one fee: 10% of the item price plus the shipping the buyer pays. There is no separate payment-processing fee, and listing is free. Your buyer separately pays a 3.6% Buyer Protection fee on the same amount — it's added to their checkout total and never comes out of your payout.

Every rate on this page was checked against Mercari's own Help Center on November 20, 2026. Sources are listed at the bottom, and the change log near the end shows what changed and when.

The catch: the 10% is charged on shipping

"10% of your price" is how most sellers model Mercari. It's wrong whenever the buyer pays shipping — which is most sales. The fee base is item price + buyer-paid shipping, so:

  • A $40 item with a $7.99 label pays a fee of $4.80 — that's 12.0% of the item price.
  • A $10 item with the same label pays $1.80 — 18.0% of the item price.

The cheaper the item, the more the shipping inflates your real rate. One thing Mercari does not charge on: sales tax. Tax is collected separately and is outside the fee base — unlike eBay, where the percentage runs on the tax-inclusive total.

Worked examples

Example 1 — a $40 sale with buyer-paid shipping

You sell a pair of shoes for $40 plus $7.99 shipping (a Mercari prepaid label, which comes out of your proceeds). The shoes cost you $15.

  • Selling fee: ($40.00 + $7.99) × 10% = $4.80
  • Payout: $47.99 − $4.80 = $43.19
  • Profit: $43.19 − $15.00 − $7.99 = $20.20
  • Your buyer also paid Buyer Protection: $47.99 × 3.6% = $1.73 (on top of their total, not from you)

Example 2 — a $25 sale with free shipping

You sell a game for $25 with free shipping; the label costs you $6.49 and the game cost you $8.

  • Selling fee: $25.00 × 10% = $2.50 (no shipping in the base — the buyer paid none)
  • Payout: $25.00 − $2.50 = $22.50
  • Profit: $22.50 − $8.00 − $6.49 = $8.01

Free shipping does reduce the fee slightly — the base shrinks — but you absorb the whole label. Whether that's a good trade depends on the item; the calculator runs both ways in seconds.

Example 3 — a $100 sale with $12.99 shipping

You sell a console accessory bundle for $100 plus $12.99 shipping. It cost you $55, and the label costs $12.99.

  • Selling fee: ($100.00 + $12.99) × 10% = $11.30
  • Payout: $112.99 − $11.30 = $101.69
  • Profit: $101.69 − $55.00 − $12.99 = $33.70
  • Buyer Protection paid by your buyer: $112.99 × 3.6% = $4.07

The flip-flop: why your old Mercari numbers may be wrong twice

Mercari has run three different fee structures in under three years:

  1. Before March 2024: sellers paid 10% + a 2.9% + $0.50 payment processing fee.
  2. March 2024 – January 2025: the experiment. Selling fee removed entirely — sellers kept 100% — while buyers paid a service fee plus processing. Mercari's own post-mortem: items became less affordable for buyers, sales dropped, and the structure "underserved buyers."
  3. Since January 6, 2025: sellers pay a flat 10% again, but the processing fee is gone for good; buyers pay a flat 3.6% Buyer Protection fee; the old $2 ACH cash-out fee was removed at the same time.

If your tracking sheet still subtracts 2.9% + $0.50 from every Mercari sale, you're understating profit on every row. If it subtracts nothing (built during the experiment), you're overstating it by 10% of price and shipping. The correct 2026 math is one line: fee = 10% × (item price + buyer-paid shipping).

Getting your money out

Earnings land in your Mercari balance once the transaction completes — the buyer has 72 hours after delivery to rate the order, and funds are released when they do. From there:

  • Direct Deposit to a checking account: free, once a day. A $2 fee applies only if a withdrawal fails (wrong account details, for example).
  • Instant Pay to an eligible Visa/Mastercard debit card: $3 flat per withdrawal, usually within 30 minutes, limited to once a day and $600 per month, and it requires government ID verification.

Cash-out fees aren't per-sale fees, but sellers who Instant Pay after every small sale quietly give back several points of margin. Batch your withdrawals.

FAQ

How much does Mercari take from a sale? 10% of the item price plus any shipping the buyer paid. Nothing else — no processing fee, no listing fee.

Does Mercari take 10% of shipping? Yes. If the buyer pays shipping, it's in the fee base. If you offer free shipping, there's no shipping in the base — but the label cost comes out of your payout instead.

What is the Buyer Protection fee? A buyer-side fee of 3.6% of item price + buyer-paid shipping, charged at checkout. It funds Mercari's cancellation and refund policy, returns on qualifying items and support. It never reduces your payout, but it does raise your buyer's all-in price.

Why does my Mercari fee look higher than 10%? Because of the shipping in the base. Divide your fee by the item price alone on a shipped order and you'll get 11–18% depending on how cheap the item was.

Are older listings on a different fee structure? Listings created before January 6, 2025 that were never updated briefly kept the old structure (no seller fee, a 10% buyer service fee plus processing) — but Mercari said existing listings would be updated automatically within a week, so by 2026 this is a rarity. If you have a genuinely untouched pre-2025 listing, check its fee in the app before relying on this page.

Does Mercari charge for listing? No. Listing is always free.
