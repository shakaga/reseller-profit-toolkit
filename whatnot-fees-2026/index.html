Whatnot Fees 2026: The Tiered Commission, Explained

The short answer: Whatnot charges two fees per sale. Commission — tiered since September 21, 2026, set by the sale's category and the tier your previous four weeks of sales unlocked (8% at Standard in most categories, 4% on coins, falling as you sell more) — is charged on the item price only. Payment processing — 2.9% + $0.30 — is charged on everything the buyer paid: item, shipping and tax.

Every rate on this page was checked against Whatnot's own Help Center on November 20, 2026. Sources are listed at the bottom, and the change log near the end shows what changed and when.

How the tiers work

Your total sales across all categories in a four-week (28-day) period set your tier for the next period. The rate charged on each sale then depends on that sale's category.

Tier Total sales in 4 weeks (US)
Standard Under $15,000
Tier 1 $15,000 – $34,999
Tier 2 $35,000 – $49,999
Tier 3 $50,000 – $99,999
Tier 4 $100,000 – $249,999
Tier 5 $250,000 – $499,999
Tier 6 $500,000 or more

Three mechanics sellers get wrong:

  1. Your rate is locked for the whole period. Crossing $15K in week 2 doesn't change what you pay in week 3 — the lower rate starts next period. The same applies in reverse when sales drop.
  2. The tier is shared, the rate is not. A seller with $10K of fashion and $5K of toys sales is Tier 1 — but the fashion sales and the toys sales are charged at their own category rates within Tier 1.
  3. Tiers reset every 28 days. Sales don't carry over or stack across periods. A big month buys you exactly one period of lower rates.

The two fees have opposite bases — that's the trap

Most marketplaces charge one percentage on the whole transaction. Whatnot splits it:

  • Commission base = item price only. Shipping and tax are excluded. On Whatnot's own example — a $10 item with $4.99 shipping and $0.85 tax — commission at 8% is $0.80, not $1.27.
  • Processing base = everything. The same order's processing is 2.9% of $15.84 plus $0.30.

A calculator (or spreadsheet) that applies one rate to one base gets at least one of the two wrong. This is also why "Whatnot takes 8%" understates your real cost: processing adds roughly 3–4 points on typical orders, more on cheap ones.

Worked examples

Example 1 — Whatnot's own example: a $10 sale with shipping and tax

You sell a card for $10. The buyer pays $4.99 shipping and $0.85 sales tax. You're a Standard-tier seller, the item cost you $4, and the buyer paid for the label.

  • Commission: $10.00 × 8% = $0.80 (item price only)
  • Processing: ($10.00 + $4.99 + $0.85) × 2.9% + $0.30 = $0.76
  • Payout: $14.99 − $0.80 − $0.76 = $13.43
  • Profit: $13.43 − $4.00 = $9.43

Example 2 — a $45 fashion sale at Tier 2

You've unlocked Tier 2 ($35K+ in the previous 4 weeks), so fashion pays 6.5% instead of 8%. You sell a jacket for $45 with free shipping; it cost you $18 and your label costs $5.50.

  • Commission: $45.00 × 6.5% = $2.93
  • Processing: $45.00 × 2.9% + $0.30 = $1.61
  • Payout: $45.00 − $2.93 − $1.61 = $40.46
  • Profit: $40.46 − $18.00 − $5.50 = $16.96

At the Standard rate the commission would have been $3.60 — the tier saved $0.67 on this one sale. Tiers matter in aggregate, not per item.

Example 3 — a $200 coin sale at Standard

Coins start at 4%, half the usual rate. You sell a coin for $200 plus $6 shipping (the buyer pays it, and the label costs you $6). The coin cost you $150.

  • Commission: $200.00 × 4% = $8.00
  • Processing: ($200.00 + $6.00) × 2.9% + $0.30 = $6.27
  • Payout: $206.00 − $8.00 − $6.27 = $191.73
  • Profit: $191.73 − $150.00 − $6.00 = $35.73

Note that processing ($6.27) nearly matches the commission ($8.00) — on coins, processing is close to half your total fee load, and no tier reduces it.

The $1,500 promotion

In select categories — Whatnot's examples include trading card games, coins and sports singles — a running promotion charges 0% commission on the portion of a single order's price above $1,500. A $2,000 TCG sale at Standard pays commission on $1,500 only: $120 instead of $160. Processing still applies to the full amount, and the promotion is time-bound, so confirm it's active in Seller Hub before pricing a high-value item around it.

Still using a flat 8%? It's wrong in both directions

Any calculator or spreadsheet built before September 21, 2026 hard-codes a flat 8% (or worse, applies it to the order total). That now overstates fees for every seller above Standard tier and every coin sale — and understates nothing, which sounds safe until you realize you're pricing and sourcing against margins that are better than you think, and passing on inventory you could have profited from. The correct 2026 math is: commission = category rate for your tier × item price only; processing = 2.9% + $0.30 × everything the buyer paid.

FAQ

How much does Whatnot take from a sale? At Standard tier, 8% of the item price (4% for coins) plus 2.9% + $0.30 processing on the buyer's total. On a $50 sale with no shipping or tax, that's $5.75 — an effective 11.5%.

Does Whatnot charge fees on shipping? Commission: no — it's on the item price only. Processing: yes — it's on item + shipping + tax.

When does my lower tier rate start? At the start of the next four-week period after the period in which you earned it. Rates are locked within a period.

Do cancelled orders count toward my tier? No. Cancelled orders are subtracted from your period total (private shows and local pickup orders don't count either). The cancellation doesn't change the rate you're paying in the current period.

Can I pick a cheaper category for my show? No — and don't. Whatnot treats deliberately miscategorized shows as fee avoidance under its policies. The category must match what you're selling.

Are Whatnot's rates the same outside the US? No. Thresholds, rates and processing vary by market (EU/UK processing is 2.42% + €/£0.25 plus VAT, for example), and Japan doesn't have tiered rates yet. This page covers the US rate card.
