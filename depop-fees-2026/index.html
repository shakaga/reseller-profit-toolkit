Depop Fees 2026: What You Actually Pay to Sell

The short answer: If you're a US-based seller, Depop charges you no selling fee at all in 2026. The 10% fee was removed in July 2024. What you actually pay per sale is:

  • Payment processing: 3.3% + $0.45 — on the item price plus shipping plus any sales tax collected
  • Boosted Listings fee: 12% — optional, only if a boosted listing sells (rate for listings boosted from 23 March 2026)
  • Your buyer pays a separate Marketplace fee (up to 5% + up to $1). It never comes out of your payout.

Every rate on this page was checked against Depop's own Help Centre on November 20, 2026. Sources are listed at the bottom, and the fee change log near the end shows what changed and when.

The fee most sellers calculate wrong: processing

The headline "0% fees" is true but incomplete. The processing fee is the real per-sale cost, and its trap is the base: Depop charges 3.3% on everything it processes for you — the item price, the shipping the buyer paid, and any sales tax collected — then adds $0.45.

That has two consequences:

  1. Free shipping isn't fee-free. If you charge $7.95 shipping on a $60 item, you're paying processing on $67.95, not $60. If you bake shipping into the price instead, same total, same fee — there's no arbitrage either way.
  2. Cheap items get hit hardest. The flat $0.45 doesn't scale down. On an $8 item with free shipping, processing is $0.71 — an effective rate of 8.9% — before you've paid for the item or postage. Low-priced, high-volume sellers feel Depop's fees the most, which is the opposite of what "0% fee" suggests.

Worked examples

Example 1 — a $35 sale, free shipping, no Boost

You sell a vintage tee for $35. It cost you $12, and the shipping label costs you $6.50 (the buyer pays no shipping).

  • Processing: $35.00 × 3.3% + $0.45 = $1.61
  • Payout: $35.00 − $1.61 = $33.39
  • Profit: $33.39 − $12.00 − $6.50 = $14.89

Example 2 — a $60 boosted sale

You sell a jacket for $60 plus $7.95 shipping (your label costs $7.95). The item cost you $25, and the sale came through a Boosted Listing (you used a Depop label, so the Boost fee applies to the item price only).

  • Processing: ($60.00 + $7.95) × 3.3% + $0.45 = $2.69
  • Boost fee: $60.00 × 12% = $7.20
  • Payout: $67.95 − $2.69 − $7.20 = $58.06
  • Profit: $58.06 − $25.00 − $7.95 = $25.11

Note what Boost did: without it, profit would have been $32.31. Boost only makes sense if the listing wouldn't have sold — or would have sold much later — without it.

Example 3 — a $15 sale with buyer-paid shipping

You sell a top for $15 plus $4.50 shipping; the label costs you $4.50 and the item cost $5.

  • Processing: ($15.00 + $4.50) × 3.3% + $0.45 = $1.09
  • Payout: $19.50 − $1.09 = $18.41
  • Profit: $18.41 − $5.00 − $4.50 = $8.91

The Boost fee change (23 March 2026)

Boosted Listings is Depop's paid promotion: your item gets more visibility, and you pay a percentage only when it sells. For UK and US sellers, the fee for listings boosted from 23 March 2026 is 12%, up from 8%. Listings boosted before that date keep the 8% rate.

Two details sellers miss:

  • The Boost fee is charged on the item price (excluding tax) plus the shipping cost if you didn't use a Depop shipping label. Ship on your own label and a $60 + $7.95 sale pays Boost on $67.95 — that's $8.15 instead of $7.20.
  • Boost stacks with processing; it doesn't replace it. A boosted sale pays both.

The Marketplace fee is your buyer's fee, not yours

Since July 2024 (US), Depop has charged buyers a Marketplace fee at checkout: up to 5% of the item price plus up to $1, calculated on the item price only (excluding tax and shipping). Depop says it funds Depop Protection, support and platform improvements.

You never pay it and it's never deducted from your payout — but it does raise the total your buyer sees. A $25 item can cost your buyer up to $27.25 before shipping and tax. Worth knowing when you're pricing against the same item on a platform without a buyer fee.

Still using a calculator that takes 10%? It's wrong

The single most common Depop fee mistake in 2026 is a stale 10%. Depop removed the selling fee market by market — UK in March 2024, US in July 2024, Australia in July 2026 — but plenty of third-party calculators, blog posts and spreadsheet templates still deduct 10% from every Depop sale. If your tracking sheet does that, you've been understating your Depop profit by roughly a tenth of revenue for over two years.

The reverse mistake also exists: "0% fees" posts that forget processing entirely. The correct US seller math is: no selling fee, 3.3% + $0.45 processing on the full processed amount, 12% if boosted.

FAQ

Does Depop charge a selling fee in 2026? Not for sellers based in the US, UK or Australia — it's 0%. Sellers based elsewhere still pay 10% on all sales.

What does Depop actually take from a sale? For a US seller: the payment processing fee of 3.3% + $0.45, calculated on item price + shipping + applicable taxes. Plus 12% of the item price if the sale came through a Boosted Listing.

Why is my payout less than my item price even with "0% fees"? Because of processing — and if you offered free shipping, the label cost comes out of the same payout. Run your exact numbers in the calculator to see the split.

Do I pay the Marketplace fee? No. It's charged to your buyer at checkout (up to 5% + up to $1 in the US/UK) and never deducted from your payout.

Are fees refunded if the buyer gets a refund? Yes. Depop's Help Centre states that on a refunded sale, the selling fee, payment processing fee and boosting fee are all reversed — automatically for Depop Payments transactions.

Does Depop charge for listing items? No. There is no listing fee on Depop.
