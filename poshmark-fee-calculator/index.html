The flat $2.95 is why cheap items sting: under $15 the effective rate climbs fast — 29.5% at $10, and worse below that. The two formulas cross at exactly $14.75, where $2.95 equals 20%.

How the math works

Commission = $2.95 if the final sale price is under $15.00; 20% of the final sale price if it's $15.00 or more. "Final" matters: the fee is charged on the price after the discount or accepted offer, not your original list price.

Shipping never enters the fee. The buyer pays $6.49 for a prepaid label covering up to 5 lbs. If you offer a shipping discount to close a sale, Poshmark deducts that discount from your earnings — enter it above to see the real payout.

Profit = sale price − commission − shipping discount offered − item cost − your other shipping costs.

FAQ

How much does Poshmark take from a $50 sale?
$10.00 — the 20% commission. You keep $40.00 before your item cost. There is no processing fee on top.
Why did Poshmark take $2.95 from my $10 sale instead of $2.00?
Because sales under $15 don't pay 20% — they pay the $2.95 flat fee. On anything under $14.75, the flat fee is actually more than 20% would be.
Is the fee charged on my list price or the offer price?
The final sale price. Accept a $32 offer on a $40 listing and the 20% is charged on $32.
Do I pay for shipping on Poshmark?
Not normally — the buyer pays the $6.49 flat label. You pay only if you offer a shipping discount (deducted from your earnings), ship something over 5 lbs (label upgrade: $5 for 5–10 lbs, $10 for 10–15 lbs), or use the wrong USPS packaging ($5 fee).
Does Poshmark charge a buyer fee?
Not under the current structure. The October 2024 experiment with buyer-side fees was rolled back within three weeks; buyers pay the item price, shipping and sales tax only.
